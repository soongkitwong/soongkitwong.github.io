About

Research at the intersection of information, identity, and choice.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in economics at [University Name]. My research asks how people use information from others when outcomes are uncertain and when some sources feel more identity-relevant than others.

The broader agenda is about a practical problem in applied microeconomics: observed choices may not cleanly reveal stable preferences when people differ in what they know, whom they trust, or which options they consider relevant.

Job Market Paper

Identity and Social Learning in Risky Choice

Experimental economics; social information; identity; risky choice

This paper studies whether shared national identity changes social learning in risky choice. In a within-subject experiment, participants first make baseline lottery choices and later observe referent choices under conditions that vary referent nationality and access to direct lottery information.

The design separates three objects that are often bundled together in field settings: the social signal itself, whether the signal comes from an identity-relevant source, and whether participants can evaluate the underlying option directly.
